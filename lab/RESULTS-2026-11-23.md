# Physical-device qualification — 2026-11-23

The Pixel 6 (Android 17 / API 37) and networked iPhone 12 mini (iOS 27.2.1, build
24B156) ran separate `dev.daybrite.syncdevicelab` installations against an authenticated,
disposable HTTPS/WebDAV server. Both used native networking, real SQLite databases, and
the working-tree sync engine. All synchronized content was synthetic.

## Failure found and fixed

The first concurrent-compaction campaign failed on its third round. The iPhone published
a complete replacement snapshot and deleted old transaction files after the Pixel had
listed them. The Pixel downloaded four transactions, then received HTTP 404 for the fifth
and returned `Store(NotFound)`. Its database passed SQLite integrity checking, with four
unapplied records waiting. No acknowledged data loss was observed.

Sync and explicit compaction now refresh the listing and snapshot scan at most twice when
a listed raw record disappears. A record absent from the refreshed listing must be covered
by validated state before proceeding. Genuine missing history still fails; cleanup and
publication budgets remain shared across retries.

The deterministic [regression tests](../crates/day-sync/tests/listing_race.rs) fail against
the saved pre-fix source and pass with the correction. They cover disappearance at the
first and third GET, both sync and compaction, genuine loss without a replacement, and
bounded handling of a listing that repeatedly advertises an unreadable record.

The earlier lost-upload-receipt/known-snapshot pruning fix was also exercised on both
phones. Neither finding establishes the cause of the original Fleet Reader incident,
whose underlying error was not retained.

## Coverage limits

This qualifies the engine and native transport against the disposable fixture, not universal
100% reliability. Fleet Reader's actual feed adapter on the devices, automatic/background
scheduling, credential lifecycle, OS network-policy changes, direct Fastmail behavior,
long-duration provider outages, and broad device/OS coverage remain separate qualification
work. Fleet's core tests do not substitute for those device-level checks.

Day UI scripting could not complete its engine connection (Android EOF; iOS unreachable
port). The Pixel screen was captured and inspected directly. No automated UI smoke-test pass
is claimed; the independent protocol controller and device assertions completed successfully.
